**Abstract** — Stroke-induced somatosensory deficits disrupt sensorimotor integration (SMI) and limit motor recovery, yet the immediate network-level effects of different somatosensory interventions remain unclear. We propose an informatics framework based on edge-centric dynamic connectivity (ECDC) to characterize transient, whole-brain EEG network responses to sensory-level focal vibratory stimulation (FVS) and neuromuscular electrical stimulation (NMES). In 15 chronic stroke participants and 15 healthy controls, FVS induced shorter global theta-network latency, higher contralesional alpha-network diversity, and reduced theta–alpha phase–amplitude coupling compared with NMES, providing interpretable network signatures for differentiating stimulation-specific brain responses and a data-driven route toward personalized SMI rehabilitation.
